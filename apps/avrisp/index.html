AVR ISP Programmer

Program classic AVR microcontrollers (ATtiny, ATmega, AT90, 175 types) over their ISP header with a USBasp or a USBtinyISP. Write BIN and HEX files, erase the chip and set fuses and lock bits, with every fuse field explained.

This browser cannot access USB devices. Open this page in Chrome, Edge or Opera on a desktop computer.

Firmware

    Choose Flash firmware and select the programmer. The chip is erased, the firmware written and verified, as avrdude -U flash:w does.

    Fuses and lock bits

    Read the chip info first; the fields of its fuses then appear here.

    Change a field or type a hex value. Settings that disable the reset pin, turn on debugWIRE, select an external clock or lock the chip ask for confirmation: after them, ISP may need a high-voltage programmer, the external clock, or a chip erase.

    Chip

    Detects the chip by its signature and reads fuses, lock bits and calibration. Changes nothing.

    Erases flash and EEPROM (unless EESAVE is set) and removes the lock bits. Fuses stay.

    Wiring

    Connect the programmer's ISP cable to the chip (pin 1 is marked on the plug):

      6-pin ISP              10-pin ISP (USBasp)
 MISO  1 ● ● 2  VCC     MOSI  1 ● ● 2  VCC
  SCK  3 ● ● 4  MOSI          3 ● ● 4  GND
  RST  5 ● ● 6  GND      RST  5 ● ● 6  GND
                         SCK  7 ● ● 8  GND
                        MISO  9 ● ● 10 GND

    The programmer can power the chip through its power jumper; many USBasp clones also select 5 V or 3.3 V with a jumper. The ISP clock (SCK) must be below a quarter of the chip's clock: new chips run at 1 MHz, so the default clocks work; for chips running at 128 kHz choose a slow SCK under More options (USBasp with old firmware: close the slow-clock jumper JP3).

    Programmer

    No programmer chosen

    USBasp (16C0:05DC) or USBtinyISP (1781:0C9F).

    More options

    Windows: the browser needs the WinUSB driver. Plug in the programmer, start Zadig, choose Options, List All Devices, select USBasp or USBtiny and install WinUSB. Tools that expect the libusb-win32 or libusbK driver may then need that driver again.

    Linux: allow access to the programmers:

    echo 'SUBSYSTEM=="usb", ATTR{idVendor}=="16c0", ATTR{idProduct}=="05dc", MODE="666"' | sudo tee /etc/udev/rules.d/99-avrisp.rules
echo 'SUBSYSTEM=="usb", ATTR{idVendor}=="1781", ATTR{idProduct}=="0c9f", MODE="666"' | sudo tee -a /etc/udev/rules.d/99-avrisp.rules
sudo udevadm control --reload-rules

    macOS: no driver needed.
