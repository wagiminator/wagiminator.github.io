Gerber Panelizer

Puts several boards, or many copies of one board, onto a panel: held by tabs with mouse bites or separated by V-score, with a frame, fiducials and tooling holes. The result is a ZIP with Gerber and Excellon files for the board house. Your files stay in your browser.

      Files

        Good to know

        • Files: Gerber RS-274X and X2 from KiCad, EasyEDA, Eagle, Altium and others, with Excellon drill files. The layers are found by name and by the X2 attributes; plated and not plated holes stay apart. Inner layers of multilayer boards are kept.
        • Tabs join the boards with each other and with the frame across the milled gap. The board house mills along the outline, which runs around boards, tabs and frame. 2 mm spacing is what most board houses mill.
        • Mouse bites are rows of small holes where a tab meets a board, so it breaks cleanly. "Into board" moves them a little inside the board edge, so nothing is left standing out after breaking.
        • V-score needs rectangular boards of the same size: the board house cuts V-grooves from both sides along straight lines. Ask your board house which layer it wants the score lines on.
        • Frame: rails for handling and for the assembly machine, with fiducials (bare copper dots) and tooling holes.
        • Ordering: board houses often count each design on a panel and may charge extra for panels made by the customer; check their rules before you order.
