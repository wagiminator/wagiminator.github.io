Solder Paste Stencil

Makes a solder paste stencil to print yourself from the Gerber files of a board: the paste openings in a thin plate, a frame that holds the board in place and pins that go into its holes. As STL for the 3D printer, or as SVG for a laser cutter or cutting plotter. With the Interactive BOM for placing the parts, the board is ready for the hot plate or oven.

Stencil

Open the Gerber files of a board: the ZIP as it goes to the board house, or the single files together. The paste layer and the board outline are needed; drill files give the holes for the pins.

    Files

    Good to know

    • Printing: the STL lies as it is printed, the plate flat on the bed and frame and pins upwards; turned over, it goes onto the board. A resin printer gives the finest openings (down to about 0.3 mm); with a filament printer, a thin nozzle and a plate of two layers, pitches from about 0.8 mm work.
    • Thickness: 0.10 to 0.15 mm for fine pitch, up to 0.2 mm for larger parts. A thicker plate gives more paste.
    • Openings come from the paste layer of the Gerber files, smoothed to the size the printer can make. Openings narrower than 0.25 mm are pointed out.
    • Frame and pins: the frame with a pocket in the shape of the board holds it in place; pins in mounting holes or other holes keep the stencil from moving. Pins need holes without paste.
    • Bottom side: the stencil is made as seen from the bottom, mirrored against the top view of the board.
    • SVG: the plate outline and the openings in millimetres at 1:1, for cutting Kapton or Mylar foil with a laser cutter or cutting plotter.
